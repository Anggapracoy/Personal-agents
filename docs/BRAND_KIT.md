# Dash brand kit

## Product name

The product is **Dash**, capital D, across web, iPhone, notifications, sharing, permissions and marketing. The App Store and TestFlight listing name is **Hey Dash**, with a space. The installed iPhone Home Screen name and in-app name remain **Dash**. The public website comes from `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_ORIGIN`; support links use `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPPORT_EMAIL`. Configure both for each installation. Keep the approved monochrome mascot and conversation characters. Never use the retired lettermark as the favicon or project icon. Runtime compatibility keys and historical design references are not display branding. This source distribution uses example Apple identifiers; configure your own identifiers consistently before signing.

## Product framing

Dash is a personal assistant that notices what needs attention and helps get it done. People can text it a task anytime. Explain both parts with concrete examples: a renewal spotted in connected email, a schedule clash, or a booking requested in chat. Lead with the assistant and the work it handles. Do not call it an AI agent, mention AI in product framing, or use em dashes in user-facing copy. Avoid generic “Ask anything” positioning and promises of involvement at every step. Explain that Dash asks when it needs details, sign-in, or approval and reports the outcome. Keep accurate data-sharing disclosures and legal information.

Landing and sharing headline: “Your assistant. Less on your plate.” Sign-in: “Meet your assistant. Less on your plate.” At standard text sizes, break after “assistant.” to create two balanced lines; allow natural wrapping at accessibility sizes. Onboarding: “Your personal assistant.” These express the same promise rather than separate product identities.

## Product voice

Dash gets things done for one person. Short, concrete sentences. Say what needs you, what is happening, and what finished. A pinned request is a question of eight words or fewer: “Rain at 6. Move inside?”, “Delta $189. Book?”. A receipt is one line of fact: “Booked. Sunday 5pm, for 4.” Only claim an action finished when the run result says so.

Typing dots appear only while final assistant response text is being written. Reading, reasoning, and tool use never insert working or tool rows into the transcript; quiet header status carries activity.

Show each finished assistant message all at once; do not reveal its text token by token. Deliver the completed message promptly after it is saved.

## Visual direction

This file documents the current approved design. The foundations and shared component tables below are canonical; feature sections describe behavior and explicit exceptions. Update the canonical rule when a design changes instead of appending a competing rule. Historical mockups are references, not release specifications. Implementation: `app/wdyt.css`, `app/home.tsx`, `app/composer.tsx`, `app/task-screen.tsx`, `app/approvals.tsx`, `app/task-icon.tsx`.

One adaptive canvas (white / black), one system typeface, a compact role-based type scale, and hairlines instead of unnecessary boxes. Colour belongs to the approved character palette, state indicators, outgoing messages and the blue send control. Do not introduce silver or yellow control themes; the approved golden character and amber attention state remain valid.

## Shared components

Reuse the established component for the same role. A metadata badge is not a button, and a conversation title is not a metadata badge. These intentional variants preserve the current interface; do not make them visually identical or create additional variants for individual screens.

### Badges

The **metadata badge** (`.wd-for-you`) uses canvas fill, a 1 px outline mixed from 18% ink and 82% canvas, and text mixed from 70% ink and 30% canvas. Use the metadata type role, 0 px vertical / 6 px horizontal padding, and 10 px radius. Keep it inline beside the title with a 7 px gap; retain the badge and truncate an overlong title. It is noninteractive and does not look selected. Both themes derive from shared ink/canvas colors.

“For you” identifies unanswered daily personal suggestions by morning suggestion provenance. Do not infer it from title text or generic proactive status; email and calendar alerts remain untagged. Remove it when the suggestion becomes an ordinary conversation, not merely when opened. State dots and message reactions retain their separate semantic treatments.

### Surfaces and motion

Use canvas for pages, soft surfaces for contained content, hairlines for quiet separation, and existing glass for floating controls, composers and conversation titles. Shapes follow the component above rather than one universal pill radius. Reserve shadows for floating elements. Use the documented interaction-specific duration or spring; reduced motion removes spatial transitions. Keep 44 × 44 px / pt minimum interactive targets and do not apply them to noninteractive metadata badges.

Newly received agent bubbles arrive with a restrained 300 ms lift and slight spring settle. Play this only when a message joins an already open conversation, including photo, video and file messages; opening an existing chat does not replay it. Reduce Motion shows the bubble immediately.

Inline conversation controls transition into their compact receipts in the same timeline position. Crossfade the outgoing control and incoming receipt while resizing the shared slot over about 210 ms; keep the old visual noninteractive during the transition. ## Website sign-in handoff

`browser_request_signin` shows “Sign in to {host}”. On iPhone the native `SignInSheet` opens the site in an isolated `WKWebsiteDataStore.nonPersistent()` browser; on Done the session cookies for that site are sent once to `POST /api/runs/[id]/signin`, imported into the cloud browser, and the thread resumes. The handoff cookies are imported into the account-scoped Browserless browser and may be saved in its authenticated profile for later sessions; do not claim cookies are never persisted. Copy: “You sign in on {host} here. The signed-in session is passed to your assistant. Your password isn’t stored.” Do not claim more than that. On desktop the remote browser sheet is the fallback.
